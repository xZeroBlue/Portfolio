Projects / Second and third year

FPGA & digital electronics

Two projects in Verilog on an Intel DE10-Lite FPGA board, going from individual logic gates up to reading real sensors and generating signals checked on an oscilloscope.

Language
Verilog HDL
Tools
Quartus Prime, ModelSim
Board
DE10-Lite
Verification
Simulation + oscilloscope

Workflow

Every design followed the same disciplined path, which is how digital hardware is developed in industry:

  1. Write Verilog
  2. →
  3. Simulate in ModelSim
  4. →
  5. Synthesise in Quartus
  6. →
  7. Assign pins
  8. →
  9. Test on hardware

Simulating first caught logic errors before they reached the board; testing on hardware then exposed the real-world issues, such as pin assignments, that simulation can't.

Arithmetic processing unit

I designed an arithmetic processing unit (APU) built up from logic gates, to understand how a microprocessor actually performs arithmetic and logic operations rather than treating it as a black box.

Sensor interfacing

I connected three sensors to the FPGA and turned their raw readings into values a person can read on the board's seven-segment hex displays, including alphanumeric characters:

  • ADXL345 accelerometer: measuring acceleration and tilt.
  • Onboard gyroscope: measuring rotation.
  • Ultrasonic distance sensor: measuring distance in centimetres.

Waveform generator

I generated four different waveforms, selectable with the board's switches, and checked each one's shape and timing on an oscilloscope to confirm the hardware matched the simulation. The animated trace on the home page is a nod to this project.
